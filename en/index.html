About me

MY STORY

My journey in sport began with boxing. In school I was overweight, which meant I constantly faced bullying and couldn't stand up for myself. That's why I decided to take up boxing. I trained for about a year and a half and had already started thinking about my first competitions, but after the war began I had to move to another city.

That's where I first walked into a gym. At first it was simply a wish to keep doing sport, but very quickly I realized I enjoyed training in the gym far more than boxing.

At that time I was choosing which field to develop in and which profession to pick. One day I heard a quote from billionaire Igor Rybakov: "How do you know if a profession is your calling? Ask yourself: what would you do if you had enough money for everything you need. If you find the answer — that is most likely your calling." And that is when I realized I wanted to become a trainer.

After that I started training with the best specialists in Ukraine, constantly self-educating, studying anatomy, physiology, biomechanics, sports nutrition and the specifics of training with various health conditions, and then applying all this knowledge in practice.

Right now I've been working as a full-time personal trainer for over 2 years. During this time 50+ clients have gone through my hands, and practically each of them came with their own story, goals and specifics.

I've worked with people who have spinal hernias and protrusions, various degrees of scoliosis, thyroid conditions, menstrual cycle disorders, vision problems, diastasis, lipid metabolism disorders, liver disease, a client after the removal of one kidney who was on regular dialysis, people who had recovered from anorexia, and many other conditions requiring an individual approach.

Another area I'm genuinely interested in is competitive bodybuilding. I've worked with athletes close to competition-level shape. I'm well versed in building muscle proportions for different competitive categories, mass-gain strategies, fat loss, cutting, contest prep and peak week, as well as supporting athletes who use sports pharmacology.

What matters most to me now is that every person who comes to me doesn't just get a training program, but is able to change their body, improve their health, and learn to maintain that result on their own.

Client results — full stories

+10 KG OF QUALITY MASS IN 6 MONTHS

Gaurav · No calorie counting · Vegetarian diet

Where he started

Gaurav came back to the gym after a long break. He'd trained before, but had never actually managed to get a real result.

  • a lumbar disc hernia — serious pain, sometimes hard to even move;
  • his back ached pretty much all the time;
  • a sensitive stomach that couldn't handle most sports nutrition;
  • vegetarian, no meat or eggs.

He wanted one thing — gain muscle, strengthen his back, and stop feeling it every single day.

What we did

We built a program around three sessions a week and paced the load so his back could actually recover while he still kept progressing. For nutrition, we skipped calorie counting — just worked out what and how much to eat, in a way that made sense and didn't feel like a chore.

We picked supplements he tolerated well to cover protein and energy, and added core and lower-back stability work.

6 months later

  • +10 kg of mass, with barely any fat gained;
  • his whole body feels noticeably stronger;
  • the back pain is basically gone;
  • his back no longer holds him back — in life or in the gym;
  • solid habits that stick on their own, in both training and eating.

We've now been working together for over a year and a half. Once the main goal was reached, we shifted into maintaining his shape and feeling good.

  • And through all of it, not a single moment where his vision was at risk.

Despite serious vision restrictions, he safely gained over 10 kg of muscle, got rid of his back pain, and built a strong, athletic physique.

TRAINING AFTER SURGERY WITH SEVERE SCOLIOSIS

Kostia · After spinal surgery · safe training

Where he started

Kostia came to me after surgery for severe scoliosis. Despite the surgery and the titanium hardware in his spine, the curve was still there. His previous trainer had given him a program loaded with axial spinal load, even though his doctor had told him directly to avoid that kind of exercise entirely.

What we did

  • Completely rebuilt his training program.
  • Cut out anything that loads the spine axially.
  • Added safe core work to support the spine.
  • Cleaned up his technique.

Result

  • Moved to a fully safe training process.
  • Training actually became enjoyable.
  • The program matches exactly what his doctor recommended.
  • After the consultation he kept training on his own, following the plan.

Even with such a complex spinal condition, we managed to build a safe training system — one that supports his health, still gives him progress, and doesn't kill his motivation to train.

RELEASED MUSCLE TENSION AND MOVED INTO STEADY MASS GAIN

Maks · Tight chest muscle · Tense thighs

Starting point

Maks came to me with the goal of gaining muscle mass. But by the first few sessions it became clear we first had to deal with a different problem.

  • his right chest muscle was significantly tight;
  • strong tension in the front thigh muscles;
  • this made even a basic bodyweight squat difficult.

What we did

We added stretching, self-massage and work with an MFR roller and a massage gun to the program. I showed him how to release tight muscles on his own at home. Maks also visited a massage therapist once to pinpoint the problem areas more precisely, and from there we worked fully according to our own plan.

Result

  • Within a few months the muscle tension was fully released.
  • Normal mobility was restored.
  • Training became comfortable.
  • Maks was then able to progress properly in the gym and gained a solid amount of muscle mass.

A clear example of how, before chasing mass, it's sometimes more important to first sort out mobility and tension — without that, progress simply hits a ceiling.
